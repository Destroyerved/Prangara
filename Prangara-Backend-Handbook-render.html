3. The Exact Mathematical Calculations (No Black Box)

A. Scope 2: Purchased Electricity Calculation

Purchased grid electricity emissions are calculated using state and regional CEA baseline factors:

$$E_{Scope2} = \frac{\text{Electricity (kWh)} \times \text{Grid Factor } (tCO_2e/MWh)}{1000}$$ Example: $500,000 \text{ kWh} \times 0.716 \text{ kg/kWh} / 1000 = \mathbf{358.00 \text{ tCO}_2\text{e}}$

B. Scope 1: Thermal Fuel Aggregation & NCV Energy Conversion

Different fossil fuels (Indian coal, biomass briquettes, furnace oil, natural gas) have different energy densities. Prangara normalizes them all into **Gigajoules (GJ)** using Net Calorific Values (NCV):

Fuel KeyNet Calorific Value (NCV)IPCC Emission FactorStandard Unit
COAL_INDIAN15.9 GJ / tonne94.6 kg CO2e / GJMetric Tonne
BIOMASS_BRIQUETTE15.0 GJ / tonneExempt from Scope 1 total (Biogenic)Metric Tonne
FURNACE_OIL0.0405 GJ / kg77.4 kg CO2e / GJKilograms
NATURAL_GAS0.0360 GJ / Sm356.1 kg CO2e / GJStandard m³
DIESEL0.0360 GJ / Litre74.1 kg CO2e / GJ (2.68 kg CO2/L)Litre
$$\text{Thermal Energy (GJ)} = \sum \left( \text{Fuel Qty}_i \times \text{NCV}_i \right)$$ $$\text{Process Heat Emissions } (tCO_2e) = \sum \left( \text{Fuel Qty}_i \times \text{Factor}_i \right)$$ Critical GHG Protocol Rule: Biomass briquette direct CO₂ emissions are biogenic. Under GHG Protocol, they are excluded from the Scope 1 total and reported on a transparent separate memo disclosure line: $\text{Biogenic } CO_2 = Qty \times 1.55 \text{ tCO}_2/\text{t}$.

4. How Prangara "Predicts" & Diagnoses (The Heuristic Logic)

The Gate-to-Gate vs. Cradle-to-Gate Invariant

A fundamental mistake in amateur carbon calculators is comparing total factory emissions against published energy benchmarks. Prangara enforces a strict separation:

Gate-to-Gate Intensity (Scope 1 + Scope 2):
$\frac{\text{Scope 1} + \text{Scope 2}}{\text{Annual Production (Tonnes)}}$
This represents what the plant operations actually control inside their four walls (boilers, furnaces, motors). This is the ONLY part benchmarked against sector peers.
Cradle-to-Gate Footprint (Scope 1 + 2 + 3):
$\frac{\text{Scope 1} + \text{Scope 2} + \text{Scope 3}}{\text{Annual Production (Tonnes)}}$
Includes purchased raw materials, packaging, and freight. A plant that purchases more steel per unit is not running a worse machine shop; mixing the two produces false penalties.

The 3 Benchmark-Relative Leak Detection Rules

In backend/engine/leaks.py, a "leak point" is not a physical gas leak—it is a point where financial capital and carbon escape unnecessarily relative to industry peers:

Rule NameTrigger ConditionSeverity AssignedReal-World Industrial Example
1. Benchmark Breach Plant's stream intensity exceeds the 75th percentile (p75) of sector peers. Critical / High A textile dyeing unit consumes 780 kWh/t when the Surat cluster p75 is 620 kWh/t (indicating low-efficiency motors or pump throttling).
2. Material Concentration Stream represents > 15% of total footprint and sits above the 50th percentile (p50 median). Moderate A foundry's cupola furnace coal consumption is 28% of total emissions and slightly above peer median (high-impact optimization candidate).
3. Structural Hotspot Stream exceeds > 25% of total footprint with no benchmark available (Scope 3 materials). Watch An auto-parts stamper whose virgin CRCA steel sheets represent 72% of total emissions (action: substitute secondary scrap steel).

5. Marginal Abatement Cost Economics (MACC Engine)

Located in backend/engine/macc.py. How does the backend rank what a factory should do first? It calculates the Levelized Cost of Abatement (LCOA):

$$LCOA = \frac{\left( CRF \times \text{Capex} \right) + \Delta \text{Opex} - \text{Gross Annual Saving}}{\text{Annual Abatement } (tCO_2e)}$$

1. Capital Recovery Factor (CRF): The Honest Way to Annualize Capex

Amateur calculators charge 100% of Capex to Year 1, making a 25-year solar plant look financially terrible compared to cheap LED bulbs. Prangara annualizes capital using the discount rate $r$ (default 12% MSME cost of capital) and asset life $n$:

$$CRF = \frac{r(1+r)^n}{(1+r)^n - 1}$$ Example: At 12% discount rate over 15-year life: $CRF = \frac{0.12(1.12)^{15}}{(1.12)^{15} - 1} = \mathbf{0.1468}$
A ₹50 Lakh solar plant has an annualized capital charge of: $50,00,000 \times 0.1468 = \mathbf{₹7,34,000 / \text{year}}$.

2. Net Annual Benefit & Simple Payback

$$\text{Net Annual Benefit (₹/yr)} = \text{Gross Energy Saving} - \left( CRF \times \text{Capex} \right) - \Delta \text{Opex}$$ $$\text{Simple Payback (Years)} = \frac{\text{Capex}}{\text{Gross Annual Saving} - \Delta \text{Opex}}$$

3. The Golden MACC Curve Ranking Rule

  • Negative LCOA ($< 0$): The intervention generates more annual cash savings than its annualized cost. It puts money in the owner's bank account every single year.
  • Positive LCOA ($> 0$): Deep decarbonization interventions (e.g. electric boilers, carbon capture) that require net capital subsidy.

7. Export Exposure Math: EU CBAM & SEBI BRSR

Located in backend/engine/assess.py (function _compliance). How does the backend predict cross-border export penalties?

$$\text{Embedded Operational Emissions } (tCO_2e) = \text{Scope 1} + \text{Scope 2}$$ $$\text{Exported Embedded Carbon } (tCO_2e) = (\text{Scope 1} + \text{Scope 2}) \times \text{EU Export Share } (\%)$$ $$\text{Indicative CBAM Annual Exposure (₹)} = \text{Exported Embedded Carbon} \times \text{Reference Carbon Price (₹7,000/tCO}_2\text{e)}$$
Why Upstream Scope 3 is Excluded: CBAM rules require certified primary supplier data for precursor materials. Guessing upstream supply chain emissions would be presenting an estimate as a verified liability, which would fail customs scrutiny.
